# Recap — from the raw activity to the HMM and the geometry

*Written 2026-07-16. A plain-language synthesis of where the analysis stands, aimed at both
experimental and computational readers. It links three things: what the **raw activity**
looks like (PSTHs, rasters), what the **HMM** says about it, and what the **dimensionality
reduction** says about it. The full technical version is
[`where_we_are_HMM_rSLDS_dimred.md`](where_we_are_HMM_rSLDS_dimred.md); the executive status
list is [`project_synthesis_2026-07-15.md`](project_synthesis_2026-07-15.md). Ask for any
section to be expanded.*

> as broken links on GitHub or a fresh clone until the figures are regenerated. Each figure

**The question.** Is a decision a **temporal sequence of stereotyped population states**,
**shared across brain areas** but drawn on **area-specific geometry** — rather than a clean
relay from sensory to motor cortex? We test it on three regions along a visual→motor axis:
**VISp** (primary visual cortex), **SCm** (motor superior colliculus), **MRN** (midbrain
reticular nucleus), recorded simultaneously in the IBL Brain-Wide Map.

---

## 1. The starting point — what the raw activity already shows

Before any model, the data-inspection notebook (`00_data_smoketest.ipynb`) and the
preprocessing-summary figures already show the key phenomena by eye.

**The three areas respond differently in shape.** Population PSTHs (average firing rate over
time, ± SEM across trials): **SCm** rises and *stays up* after the stimulus, **MRN** fires a
sharp ~150–200 ms transient (median reaction time ~171 ms), **VISp** is weak and noisy.

![Population PSTH per area](../../results/figures/smoketest/psth_per_area.png)

*(The PSTH averages over neurons and trials; the band is ±1 SEM across trials. For why MRN's
band is so thin, what each area's response is time-locked to, and a 100%-contrast-only version,
see [`psth_checks_events_and_contrast.md`](psth_checks_events_and_contrast.md).)*

**There is a visible sequence of activation.** In the "macro raster" (every unit, sorted by
when it peaks, colour = firing rate), **SCm and MRN show a clean diagonal** — units switch on
one after another in a consistent order — and it repeats across all 5 sessions. **VISp's
diagonal is weaker and noisier.** This picture is the whole motivation for the HMM: a
*sequence of states*.

![Macro raster across 5 sessions × 3 areas](../../results/figures/hmm_5sessions/macro_raster_stimOn.png)

**The activity is locked to movement, not just the stimulus.** In single-neuron rasters with
trials sorted by reaction time, the response band *bends along the RT curve* (black line =
movement onset): neurons fire relative to the wheel-turn, not the flash. This is the single
most important caveat in the project (see §4).

![Single-neuron rasters sorted by reaction time](../../results/figures/smoketest/raster_example_neuron.png)

**A decoder separates two questions that are easy to confuse.** A *stimulus-side* decoder gets
better with contrast (a sensory signal); a *choice* decoder is high and roughly flat, and is
**above chance even at 0 % contrast** — the mouse commits to a turn on every trial. Decoding
"choice" and expecting it to scale with contrast is decoding the wrong label. (SCm/MRN decode
both; VISp is thin in this single session.)

![Decoding stimulus vs choice at different contrast](../../results/figures/smoketest/decoding_vs_contrast.png)

These are *observations*. The HMM and the geometry are the tools that turn them into
*measurements* with error bars and null models.

## 2. The HMM — turning "a sequence of states" into a number

**The model in one sentence.** A hidden Markov model with Poisson emissions, fit per area:
at each 40 ms bin the population is assumed to sit in one of **K** stereotyped firing-rate
patterns ("states"), and to hop between them. It answers **where** the population is and
**when it moves** — not *why*. We feed it **raw spike counts** (smoothing is forbidden here).

Two read-outs carry the science:

**(a) How many reproducible states? (state stability, not likelihood.)** Held-out likelihood
cannot choose K on spike counts — it climbs forever, because a Poisson HMM can only mimic
neural correlations by *adding a state*. Instead we test **reproducibility**: fit two HMMs on
disjoint trials, decode both on a third held-out set, and measure agreement (Adjusted Rand
Index, which ignores arbitrary state labels). The reproducible state count is **K ≈ 3 for all
three areas** (SCm supports 4 in the largest sessions). **There is no state-count gradient** —
an earlier "VISp 2 < MRN 3 < SCm 4" claim was retracted after proper model restarts. The
curves mostly *decline* from K=2 (no strong interior peak), with only a mild SCm shoulder near
K=4 — which is why K=3 is the honest common count.

![HMM state-stability scan across 5 sessions](../../results/figures/hmm_5sessions/stability_stimOn.png)

**(b) Is the traffic between states directed? (`forward_fraction`.)** Order the states by
their average time of occurrence, then count how often the population moves "forward" (to a
later state) vs "backward" on single trials. 0.5 = wandering, 1.0 = a strict forward chain.
Pooled across all 5 sessions (both stimulus- and movement-aligned):

| area | `forward_fraction` (5-session, split-half) | reading |
|---|---|---|
| **VISp** | **0.52 ± 0.03** | at chance — *undirected* (but it still switches states) |
| **SCm** | **0.64 ± 0.06** | directed in all 5 sessions |
| **MRN** | **0.64 ± 0.10** | directed on average, variable (2 sessions near chance) |

![Directedness (forward_fraction) across sessions](../../results/figures/hmm_5sessions/forward_fraction_stimOn.png)

> **The headline HMM result: motor areas traverse their states directedly; visual cortex does
> not.** This *direction* replicates across all 5 sessions and both alignments. Note the
> magnitude: often-quoted single-session values (SCm 0.83, MRN 0.79) came from one strong
> session — the honest pooled effect is **~0.64**. "Shared alphabet, area-specific sequence,"
> not "more states as you go motor."

**(c) A second, independent confirmation.** Correlating the HMM's states with the trial's task
phase (relative to a permutation null that preserves the clock) gives the **same
dissociation**: SCm and MRN track task phase above chance in 5/5 sessions, VISp only in 3/5.

## 3. Dimensionality reduction — the shape of the activity, and what drives it

Population activity lives in many dimensions; we reduce it to see its **shape** and, crucially,
to ask **what causes** the variation. Four methods, four different questions:

- **Embedding zoo (PCA, t-SNE, UMAP, …) — mostly illustration.** These make pretty pictures but
  *every* method makes a convincing one. Their quality metrics show t-SNE/UMAP preserve local
  neighbours while **destroying global distances**, so **their apparent "clusters" are not
  evidence of discrete states** — testing for discrete states is the HMM's job. PCA is the
  honest one: the first two components explain only ~25–30 % of the geometry.

- **dPCA — the method that speaks to the hypothesis.** It splits the activity into the variance
  driven by **stimulus**, by **choice/decision**, by **time**, and their mix. The project's core
  prediction comes out:

  | area | decision − stimulus variance |
  |---|---|
  | VISp | **−6.8** (stimulus-dominated) |
  | SCm | +1.8 |
  | MRN | **+6.0** (decision-dominated) |

  A clean visual→motor gradient. Caveat: this rests on one session with ~25 trials in the key
  cells, so it is a **strong lead, not yet a claim** — it needs the 5 sessions pooled.

  ![dPCA variance by cause, and the decision−stimulus gradient](../../results/figures/phase3_dimred/dpca_variance_split.png)

- **GPFA — the dynamics are slow, and slower in motor areas.** GPFA *infers* the smoothness of
  the latent trajectories instead of imposing it. Fitted timescales are **107–283 ms**,
  slowest in MRN (283 ms) — so our trajectories are not a smoothing artefact, and the motor
  areas run slower (an independent echo of the directedness result).

- **RRR / communication subspace — SCm and MRN share, they don't relay.** Reduced-rank
  regression asks through how narrow a channel one area predicts another. **MRN predicts SCm
  almost as well as SCm predicts itself** — there is *no* restricted communication subspace
  between them. Combined with the joint-PCA finding that **~42 % of the shared variance is
  cross-area covariance**, this says the two motor areas are **two readouts of a common signal**,
  not a directed loop. Read each coloured cross-area curve against the **black within-area
  control**: MRN→SCm (yellow) sits almost on SCm's own self-prediction (black).

  ![Communication subspace: cross-area vs within-area control](../../results/figures/phase3_dimred/rrr_communication.png)

## 4. The through-line — the pictures, the HMM, and the geometry agree

The three strands are not parallel; they measure the same claim in different currencies, and
they keep pointing the same way.

1. **The raster diagonal → the HMM's directedness.** The by-eye "units activate in order" in
   SCm/MRN (§1) is exactly what `forward_fraction` measures and confirms (§2b); VISp's noisy
   diagonal matches its at-chance `forward_fraction`. Same fact, once as a picture, once as a
   number with a null.

2. **The PSTH shapes → the states and their timescales.** SCm's sustained rise and MRN's sharp
   transient (§1) are the trial-averaged shadow of the metastable states the HMM finds and the
   slow contracting dynamics GPFA fits (§3).

3. **The zero-lag cross-correlogram → RRR common drive.** The raster/pynapple observation that
   SCm and MRN fire together with no lag (§1) is confirmed by RRR and joint-PCA (§3): a shared
   signal, not a relay. Three independent methods now say the same thing.

   ![SCm→MRN cross-correlogram, sharp zero-lag peak](../../results/figures/smoketest/crosscorr_SCm_MRN.png)

4. **The decoder → dPCA.** The decoder showing *choice* decodable even at 0 % contrast while
   *stimulus* scales with contrast (§1) is the same story dPCA tells by splitting variance into
   decision vs stimulus (§3): the visual→motor axis carries progressively more decision and less
   stimulus.

5. **The one caveat that qualifies all of it — the movement window.** The rasters bend along the
   RT curve (§1); quantitatively, movement has begun on ~92 % of trials by +0.8 s. So the HMM's
   late states, dPCA's "decision" variance, and the motor directedness are **entangled with
   movement and reward**. A movement-aligned control exists for exactly this reason, and **no
   state after ~0 s may be called a pure "decision" state** without it. In IBL, the choice *is*
   the wheel-turn.

## 5. Status at a glance

| finding | status |
|---|---|
| A **reliable temporal sequence** of states exists, position-dependent (not a stopwatch) | ✅ certified |
| **Motor areas traverse states directedly; visual cortex is undirected** | ✅ certified (direction; magnitude ~0.64 pooled) |
| The first "evoked" state is a **movement** state | ✅ certified (caveat, not a bug) |
| **Visual→motor gradient** (dPCA decision variance, GPFA timescale) | 🔶 strong lead — needs the 5 sessions pooled |
| **SCm↔MRN is common drive, not a feedback loop** | 🔶 lead — 3 methods agree (RRR, correlogram, HMM) |
| "We found the **integrator / line attractor**" | ❌ retracted (a smoothing artefact, not real dynamics) |
| A **state-count gradient** (2 < 3 < 4) | ❌ retracted (it is 3/4/3 — no gradient) |

**One-line synthesis.** *The areas hold a similar number of states, but the motor areas
traverse them more directedly, carry more decision variance, and run slower — a shared
sequence realised on area-specific geometry.* The single highest-value next step, which four
independent analyses point to, is to **pool the 5 replicate sessions** (add trials, don't prune
neurons) and re-measure the gradient.
