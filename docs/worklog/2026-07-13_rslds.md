`requirements.txt` said **"ssm: CONFIRMED UNINSTALLABLE (2026-07-12), do not retry"**. That was
too strong, and it was blocking the published method on a false premise. **It installs.** The
failure had *two separable causes*, both fixable:

| # | Error | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'numpy'` | legacy `setup.py` imports numpy at build time without declaring it → PEP-517 isolation starves it | `--no-build-isolation` |
| 2 | `Microsoft Visual C++ 14.0 or greater is required` | `ssm/cstats.pyx` is a C++ extension; **this laptop has no MSVC at all** | conda's **mingw-w64**, *no admin install* |

Only cause 1 had been diagnosed on 2026-07-12; the stop happened before cause 2 was even reached.
Two further sub-hurdles, both solved in `scripts/install_ssm_env.sh`:

- conda's gcc is **5.3**, old enough that `INTPTR_MAX` is invisible to C++ and `pyport.h` will not
  compile → `-D__STDC_LIMIT_MACROS`.
- conda ships **no mingw import library** for `python311.dll`, so every `Py*` symbol comes back
  `undefined reference` → generate `libpython311.a` with `gendef` + `dlltool`.

**Verified end-to-end**: builds, imports, and fits a Poisson rSLDS (`transitions="recurrent_only"`,
Laplace-EM) on the real IBL session.

> **Lesson.** "Package X doesn't install" is a *symptom*, not a diagnosis. The first error message
> hid a second, unrelated one behind it. Two errors got recorded as one permanent verdict, and a
> tested, published method was written off for a day on the strength of it.

### What is still true, and is why the env is separate

`ssm` is a **numpy-1.x** package. Installing it into `neurodatarehack` would downgrade numpy 2.3.5
and take pynapple / numba / pandas 3.0 down with it — exactly the "additive installs only" rule
from 2026-07-12. So it lives in its own env **`ndrh_ssm`**, and the notebook (in `neurodatarehack`)
calls it **out of process**: `src/dynamics/rslds_ssm.py` writes the count tensor to `.npz`, runs
`scripts/run_rslds_ssm.py` under that env's interpreter, and reads the fit back as our own
`RSLDSResult`. Nothing from `ssm` is ever imported into the working env.

Implementation note worth keeping: call the other env's **`python.exe` directly**, not `conda run`.
On Windows `conda` is a `.bat` in `condabin` that a subprocess cannot see (`WinError 2` from inside
a kernel), and `conda run` additionally refuses commands containing newlines.

## 2. Two backends, on purpose

| | `src/dynamics/rslds.py` (ours, exact EM) | `ssm` (Linderman) |
|---|---|---|
| latent | PCA on Anscombe counts, **fixed** | **inferred jointly** with the dynamics |
| emissions | Gaussian | **Poisson** |
| inference | exact forward-backward + weighted LS | Laplace-EM (**ELBO — a lower bound**) |
| cost | seconds | **~15–20 min per region** |
| role | cross-validate K, test recurrence, screen | **the fit of record** |

Why both: `ssm` is the tested, published method and is what we report — but at ~15–20 min a fit,
cross-validating K with it would take **≈13 h**, and its ELBO is a *bound* whose tightness varies
with K and D, so arg-maxing it is not model selection anyway. The fast exact-EM model does the
selection; `ssm` is fitted at the chosen K. Agreement between two very different inference schemes
is a much stronger claim than either alone.

Both return the **same `RSLDSResult`**, so every figure and statistic is backend-agnostic.

## 3. ⚠️ Ground truth killed two would-be results before they existed

`rslds.py` was validated on synthetic data with **known** attractors (3 of them, visited 0→1→2 on
every trial, Poisson spikes at an IBL-like 4 Hz).

| bin | sticky | var.exp | regime acc | switches/trial (**true 2.0**) | \|λ\|max | order-consistency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 ms | 0 | 0.24 | 0.55 | **8.3** | 1.07 | 0.84 |
| 20 ms | 4 | 0.24 | 0.84 | 2.5 | 0.98 | 1.00 |
| **50 ms** | **4** | **0.33** | **0.90** | **2.0** | **0.875** | **1.00** |

**(a) The sticky prior has to be applied in every M-step, not at initialisation.** It was being set
on the initial `log_pi` and then immediately overwritten by the first M-step — so it did nothing.
The regimes flickered (8.3 switches/trial where the truth is 2) and recovery sat at 0.55. Flickering
regimes are not metastable states, and every downstream sequence statistic would have been computed
on noise.

**(b) At 20 ms bins the latent is too noisy to identify the flow — and it fails in a way that looks
like a finding.** `|λ|max → 1.0`, so *every* regime gets classified **"line attractor / integrator"**.
That is a near-unit-root random walk fitted to shot noise, and "we found an integrator, the shape a
decision variable lives on" is exactly the paper-ready sentence it would have produced. At 50 ms the
same pipeline recovers genuinely contracting attractors (|λ| = 0.875).

> **Any integrator / line-attractor claim from the 20 ms fit is inadmissible.** This is why both
> timebases are fitted and reported side by side.

**(c) The shuffle null is not 0.5.** Within-trial bin shuffling lands at **0.53–0.62**, not 0.5,
because it still changes *which* regime is entered first. Compare `order_consistency` to its own
null, never to 0.5 — and report the **effect size**, not just p: with hundreds of trials a trivial
order bias is still "significant" (the random-order control reached p < 0.001 at an effect of
+0.06, versus +0.44 for a real sequence).

## 4. The statistic: sequence, *not* direction

`order_consistency` = mean over regime pairs of `max(P[k,l], 1 - P[k,l])`, where `P[k,l]` is the
fraction of trials in which regime *k* is first entered before *l*.

- **0.5** → the population wanders among a *set* of states in no fixed order. **No sequence.**
- **1.0** → every trial visits them in the same order. **A sequence.**

Deliberately **blind to which order**, because the claim under test is *"decision-making is a
temporal sequence of metastable states"*, not *"the sequence runs in the direction we labelled
forward"*. This is the direction-agnostic complement to `forward_fraction` (notebook 01), which
measures directedness *given* an imposed ordering.

Validated: 1.00 on the sequenced ground truth, 0.59 (effect +0.06) on a control with the **same
three attractors in random order**.

## 5. Does the rSLDS need many neurons, or many trials? — **trials**

The question that prompted this session. Parameter count:

| block | parameters | estimated from |
|---|---|---|
| emissions `C, d` | `N*D + N` | every time bin **of that neuron** |
| dynamics `A_k, b_k, Q_k` | `K(2D² + D)` | bins assigned to regime *k* |
| transitions `Π, R` | `K² + K*D` | **the switches actually observed** |

The scientific payload — the attractors and the sequence — lives in the dynamics + transitions:
≈100 parameters at D=3, K=4, estimated from the number of **regime transitions**, of which a trial
supplies only 2–4. **Trials are the currency of the switching dynamics.**

Neurons enter almost entirely through `C`, and each new neuron brings `D` parameters *plus a whole
new time series to constrain them*. They do not compete for the data that estimates the attractors —
they sharpen the per-bin estimate of `x_t`, i.e. the single-trial SNR. That is precisely what failed
at 20 ms above.

**Consequence — and it is the opposite of what the original plan assumed:**

> **Do not use MSR to prune neurons in order to "afford" the model.** It buys no statistical power
> for an rSLDS and can only cost latent SNR. The plan's framing (MSR as a way to shrink the
> population for data-hungry methods) is right for **ELA**, and **wrong for rSLDS**.
> If you want a stronger claim, **pool the 5 replicate triplet sessions** — add trials, not filters.

This lands on the same conclusion as the parallel MSR notebook, from a completely different
direction: *"Choice information is redundant across the population; the temporal trajectory is not —
it needs the whole population. Selection is fine for decoding, not for dynamics."* MSR's honest job
in notebook 03 is therefore a **robustness axis** (Part 8), not a budget.

## 6. ⚠️ The 45-minute cross-validation was a bug, not a cost

The user reported Part 4 (CV of K) taking **45+ minutes** and asked whether that was normal. It was
not. Profiling one fit at real size (843 trials × 50 bins, K=4, D=3):

| L-BFGS budget in the transition M-step | fit time | share of runtime | final LL/bin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 (what I wrote) | 40.8 s | **96%** | −0.6362 |
| 20 | 18.0 s | 93% | −0.6362 |
| **10 (now the default)** | ~9 s | — | **−0.6362** |
| 5 | 6.4 s | 80% | −0.6362 |

The transition M-step was running L-BFGS **to convergence inside every EM iteration**. That is
wasted work: EM only requires the M-step to *increase* the expected complete-data log-likelihood,
not to maximise it — the next E-step re-optimises anyway. This is textbook **generalised EM (GEM)**.

The final log-likelihood is **bit-identical** across all four budgets, so the extra 35 s per fit
bought precisely nothing. Re-verified against the synthetic ground truth: regime recovery 0.90,
|λ| 0.872, order-consistency 1.00 — **unchanged**.

Measured end result: `cross_validate_K` over K ∈ {2..6}, 3 regions, 50 ms →
**45 min → 7.4 min**. (`TRANS_MAXITER = 10` in `src/dynamics/rslds.py`.)

> **Lesson.** "This is a slow method" was the comfortable explanation and it was wrong. 96% of the
> runtime was in one call whose extra precision changed no digit of the answer. Profile before
> accepting a runtime — an optimisation nested inside an optimisation is where to look first.

## 8. K now comes from the HMM stability analysis — no model selection in the rSLDS notebook

Superseding §7's "K = 4 a priori": `notebooks/03_rslds_triplet.ipynb` now takes K **per region**
from `docs/knowledge/best_K_per_region.md` — **VISp 2, SCm 4, MRN 3** — and runs **no model
selection at all**. This is strictly better than what it replaced, because those K come from a
criterion that actually works on this data:

**Stability (chance-corrected ARI between two HMMs fitted to disjoint halves of the trials) beats
held-out likelihood here, and it is not close.** Likelihood has now failed twice in opposite
directions on the same data — argmax **K=2** off a column of `-inf`, then **K=10** off the top of
the search range. Both are boundaries, not answers. Stability asks the question likelihood cannot:
*does this state structure reproduce across independent fits?*

Consequences written into the notebook:

- **SCm K=4 is the anchor** (ARI 0.928 ± 0.003).
- **VISp is a documented negative**: ~2 reproducible states and no richer sequence (non-trivial-K
  ARI 0.680 ± **0.176**). The notebook says out loud: *if the rSLDS reports crisp multi-regime
  sequences in VISp, distrust them* — same class of error as "20 ms bins manufacture line
  attractors".
- **Part 9's job changed.** It is no longer "choose K" (stability already did, better). It is the
  **recurrence test** — `delta_recurrent`, held-out LL with R free minus R = 0 — which is the only
  thing in the notebook that distinguishes an **attractor sequence** from a **stopwatch**. Its
  likelihood-vs-K curve is kept purely as a cross-check, with a note that disagreement is evidence
  about *the criterion*, not a reason to move K.

Three caveats recorded rather than papered over:

1. Those K are **preliminary** and, by their own document's §5, **lower bounds** (`n_restarts=1`
   charges EM's bad luck to "the states are not real").
2. They were measured on a **different model at a different timebase** — Poisson HMM, 20 ms,
   rate-filtered units — and are being applied to an **rSLDS at 50 ms on all units**. HMM states and
   rSLDS dynamical regimes are not the same object. **This is a principled prior, not a
   measurement.**
3. **VISp at K=2 leaves `order_consistency` exactly one regime pair**, so it is noisy there and
   cannot express a sequence richer than "A before B". Verified it runs (1 usable pair, no crash).

Sanity check while testing the K=2 edge case: on **pure Poisson noise with no structure**, the
sequence statistic correctly **does not fire** (OC 0.679 vs null 0.638 at K=2; 0.557 vs null 0.570
at K=3). A null-data control it passes.

## 7. Notebook restructured: K fixed, CV optional

At the user's request, and it is the right order of work anyway:

- **Parts 4–8 run at a fixed `K_FIXED = 4`** (baseline → evoked → decision/movement → post-movement
  is a defensible prior, and comparable to the HMM). The science is now visible in one pass.
- **Part 9 (`RUN_CV = False` by default)** cross-validates K *and* carries the recurrence test
  (`delta_recurrent` = held-out LL with R free − with R = 0). Defaults to `CV_BINS = ["50ms"]`,
  since the 20 ms attractor read-out is inadmissible anyway (§3b) and it halves the cost.

**This is a stated shortcut, not a free lunch**, and the notebook's caveats say so: until Part 9 is
run we have **not** shown that switching depends on latent *position* rather than elapsed *time* —
and that distinction is the entire difference between an attractor sequence and a stopwatch.
