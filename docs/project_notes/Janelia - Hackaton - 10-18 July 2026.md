# My abstract
## 1st iter - Brain-wide multi population energy landscape analysis for attractors and metastable interaction  
Neural population level analysis has a growing role in the systems neuroscience research. 
The "IBL - Brain Wide Map" permits the use of a huge number of units and areas which is on a one side an incredible power but on the other side limits the type of analysis we can do on the data. In this context of large amount of data, a latent state approach could help to unveil the hidden structure of the population neural dynamics. 
- The reasoning behind is that most of the emerging cognitive function of the brain are the results of many neurons interacting in a structured manner. 
- One option to study systems with such a high number of degree of freedom, is to rely on a latent space description!
A recent data-driven framework is able to infer flexible model to describe the neural dynamics evolution through latent state variable. The framework simultaneously infers also the firing rate maps of the input neurons' spike time activity. 
This framework is well suited to describe non-stationary process like decision making and finds the attractors of the dynamics. 
 We can finally use dimensionality techniques to match the dynamics and verify the embedded latent dynamics dimension to explain the main features of the animal population neural computation. We could also use an hidden markov model as a third check of the transition using a combination of HMM and beavioural features to predict and compare.  

## 2nd iter - 
Evidences that the cognitive function as well have an embedded lower space than the units that are involved in that function are rising in the literature. However different areas seems to show different embedding and the relations between them remain poorly understood. This project will take advantage of a recently published unsupervised latent-state data-driven framework to infer models of the neural dynamics and the corresponding firing rate maps simultaneously solely from spike activity. The application of this framework on a large dataset such as the impressive "IBL - Brain Wide Map" allows to apply techniques of dimensionality reduction to search for a lower dimension embedded spaces. If the latter result to be area-specific, thus showing different "hidden geometry", this could help to understand from a dynamical system point of view the different evolution of the firing rates in the choice. Post-hoc statistical analysis can be first use to assess if the different coherence of the task are modulating the firing rate maps and introduce distortion in the geometry found. Finally, employ techniques of multi-regional interaction, such as Cell Assemblies Detection and improvements of Canonical Correlation Analysis to quantify the order and amplitude of interaction across brain areas. All together these analysis aim at shed a light from a dynamical point of view on the emergent properties of different area which are well described phenomenologically with experiments, but lack of a more theoretical structure. 

## 3rd iter - Lumo check
Evidence that cognitive functions have an embedded lower‑dimensional space than the neuronal units that implement those functions is growing in the literature. However, different areas appear to show different embeddings, and the relationships between them remain poorly understood.
This project will exploit a recently published unsupervised, latent‑state, data‑driven framework (Neuralflow) to infer neural‑dynamics models and the corresponding firing‑rate maps simultaneously, using only spike activity. Applying this framework to a large dataset (IBL - Brain Wide Map) enables statistically reliable dimensionality‑reduction techniques to search for lower‑dimensional embedded spaces.
If the resulting embeddings are area‑specific, showing distinct “hidden" geometries, this could help us understand, from a dynamical‑systems perspective, how firing‑rate trajectories evolve during choice behavior.
Post‑hoc statistical analyses can first be used to assess whether task‑related coherence modulates the firing‑rate maps and introduces distortion into the discovered geometry.
Finally, we will employ multi‑regional interaction techniques, such as Cell‑Assemblies Detection and tailored Canonical Correlation Analysis, to quantify the time order and amplitude of interactions across brain areas.
Together, these analyses aim to shed light, from a dynamical‑systems viewpoint, on the emergent properties of different areas and the interactions that gives rise to cognitive and behavioral functions.
